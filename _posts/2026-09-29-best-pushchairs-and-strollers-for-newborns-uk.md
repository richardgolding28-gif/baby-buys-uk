faq:
- question: Can a newborn go straight into any pushchair?
  answer: No — newborns lack head and neck control, so they need to be flat or fully reclined, either in a certified carrycot, a lie-flat pushchair seat, or a rear-facing car seat, until they have good head control (commonly around 6 months). Always check the specific pushchair's stated minimum age or weight before using the upright seat unit.
- question: Do I need a carrycot, or is a lie-flat seat enough?
  answer: A proper carrycot gives a fully flat, enclosed sleeping space and is generally considered the more comfortable option for very young newborns and daytime naps out and about. A lie-flat recline on the main seat unit is a lighter, more compact alternative that many pushchairs use instead, and is fine from birth provided the manufacturer states so.
- question: What UK safety standards should I check for?
  answer: Look for a pushchair or pram that meets BS EN 1888, the British/European safety standard for pushchairs and prams. If buying a travel system with a car seat, the seat itself should be UN R129 (i-Size) or ECE R44 certified and correctly fitted, ideally with an ISOFIX base rather than a seatbelt-only fitting.

A pushchair that isn't properly rated for newborn use, or that turns into a fight to fold at the school gate, causes daily frustration fast. Here's what actually matters for the newborn stage, and which ones hold up.

## What actually matters

- **Genuine from-birth suitability** — check whether this means a separate carrycot, a fully lie-flat seat recline, or an infant car seat, since newborns need to lie flat until they have good head control (generally around 6 months).
- **BS EN 1888 compliance** — the UK/European safety standard for pushchairs and prams; a car seat included in a travel system should separately carry UN R129 (i-Size) or ECE R44 certification.
- **Weight and fold size** — matters most if you're lifting it into a car boot or up steps regularly; lightweight compact-fold models are worth prioritising for smaller cars or flats.
- **How it grows with your child** — many from-birth pushchairs convert through toddlerhood, worth checking the stated maximum weight or age against how long you actually want to use it.
