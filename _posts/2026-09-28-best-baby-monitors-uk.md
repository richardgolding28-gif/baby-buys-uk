- question: Audio-only or video monitor?
  answer: Audio-only is cheaper, simpler, and has fewer things to go wrong, and is genuinely enough for most parents. Video adds real reassurance if your baby is a restless sleeper or you want to check on them without opening the door.
- question: Does Wi-Fi or non-Wi-Fi matter?
  answer: Non-Wi-Fi monitors use their own private radio connection between the two units, which some parents prefer for security and because it doesn't depend on your home internet. Wi-Fi models let you check in from a phone app anywhere, which matters more if you're often not in the house.
- question: What range do I actually need?
  answer: Most homes need far less than the advertised maximum range (which is measured in open outdoor conditions). For a typical UK house or flat, almost any monitor on the market will comfortably cover nursery-to-living-room range through walls and floors.

A baby monitor that drops connection or has a laggy, grainy picture defeats the entire point. Here's what actually matters, and which ones are reliably good.

## What actually matters

- **Connection stability** — read reviews specifically for dropouts and interference, not just picture quality out of the box.
- **Battery life on the parent unit** — a unit that needs charging every few hours becomes annoying fast; look for at least 8-10 hours between charges.
- **Night vision quality if going video** — infrared clarity varies a lot between cheap and mid-range models.
- **Two-way talk** — genuinely useful for soothing without walking to the room every time, worth checking it's included if that matters to you.
