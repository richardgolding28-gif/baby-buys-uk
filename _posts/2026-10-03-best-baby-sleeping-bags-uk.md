- question: What tog rating should I choose?
  answer: Tog depends on your room temperature, not the season by default. As a rough guide, 2.5 tog suits a typical UK bedroom at 16-20°C year-round, 1.0 tog suits warmer rooms above 21°C or summer months, and 0.5 tog or lower is for very warm conditions. The NHS and Lullaby Trust both recommend checking your baby isn't too hot (sweating, damp hair, flushed cheeks) rather than relying on tog alone, and never using a sleeping bag together with a duvet or extra blankets.
- question: At what age can my baby move from a swaddle to a standard sleeping bag?
  answer: Once your baby shows any sign of rolling, swaddling with arms restricted is no longer considered safe, and the NHS/Lullaby Trust guidance is to move to an arms-free sleeping bag at that point, typically somewhere around 8-12 weeks but it varies by baby. Transition products like Love to Dream's Swaddle Up range are designed to let one or both arms out gradually as part of that move.
- question: Is a sleeping bag safer than loose blankets?
  answer: Yes — this is consistent, non-medical safe-sleep guidance from the Lullaby Trust and NHS. A well-fitted sleeping bag of the appropriate tog removes the risk of loose bedding covering a baby's face or overheating them unevenly, provided it's the correct size (snug at the neck and armholes, with no hood) and your baby sleeps on their back with nothing else in the cot.
- question: How do I know if a sleeping bag fits properly?
  answer: Check the neck and armholes are snug enough that the bag can't ride up over your baby's face, and that there's room for hips and legs to move freely — manufacturers generally size by both weight and height, so check both against your baby's measurements rather than age alone, since babies vary a lot in size at the same age.

A sleeping bag only needs to do two things well: keep your baby at a safe, steady temperature, and not make 3am nappy changes any harder than they already are. Here's what actually matters when choosing one, and which ones are worth a look.

## What actually matters

- **Tog rating matched to your room temperature** — sleeping bags are sold by tog, not by season, so check your actual room temperature (a cheap room thermometer helps) rather than guessing from the time of year.
- **A two-way or bottom-opening zip** — lets you change a nappy without fully undressing your baby or waking them up more than necessary.
- **Correct sizing by weight and height, not just age** — most brands size by both, and a bag that's too big can let your baby wriggle down inside it, which is a safety concern.
- **No hood, and a snug (not loose) neck and armhole fit** — loose fabric around the head and neck is the main risk factor safe-sleep guidance warns against.
- **Arms-free design once your baby can roll** — the NHS and Lullaby Trust advise moving out of any arms-restricted swaddle as soon as there are early signs of rolling; this is general safe-sleep guidance, not medical advice specific to your baby.
- **Easy-care fabric** — nights go wrong often enough that machine-washable, tumble-dry-safe cotton is worth prioritising over anything that needs special care.
