faq:
- question: Will an anti-colic bottle actually stop my baby's colic?
  answer: No bottle can promise that, and colic has several possible causes, only some of which relate to swallowed air during feeds. Anti-colic bottles are designed to reduce the air a baby takes in while feeding, which can ease wind, gas, and some reflux-like symptoms for many babies, but they are not a medical treatment and won't resolve colic that has another cause.
- question: When should I speak to a health visitor or GP instead of just switching bottles?
  answer: If your baby has persistent crying for long stretches, feeding difficulties, poor weight gain, vomiting beyond normal spit-up, or you're simply worried, speak to your health visitor or GP rather than relying on a bottle change alone. This guide is about feeding equipment, not medical advice, and it doesn't replace that conversation.
- question: Do all anti-colic bottles need a specific teat flow rate?
  answer: Yes — most anti-colic bottle systems are sold with a range of teat flow rates (newborn/slow, medium, fast) and it's worth matching the flow to your baby's age and feeding pace rather than assuming the bottle that comes in the starter pack is the right one long-term. A flow that's too fast can itself cause gulping and more swallowed air.
- question: Is it worth paying more for a wide-neck bottle over a standard-neck one?
  answer: Wide-neck bottles are generally easier to fill, clean, and add formula powder to without spilling, and many anti-colic systems are built specifically around a wide-neck design. It's a convenience difference more than a performance one — standard-neck anti-colic bottles still work the same way internally.

Not every grizzly evening is colic, and no bottle is a guaranteed fix — but if swallowed air during feeds is part of what's unsettling your baby, a well-designed anti-colic bottle can genuinely help. Here's what actually matters when choosing one, and which ones are worth a look.

## What actually matters

- **A proper venting or valve system, not just a "anti-colic" label** — look for a described internal vent, valve, or straw system that actively channels air away from the teat, rather than the word alone on the packaging.
- **Teat flow rate matched to your baby's age** — most ranges offer slow, medium, and fast flow teats; too fast a flow can cause gulping, which works against the point of an anti-colic design.
- **Number of parts and ease of cleaning** — some anti-colic systems use extra internal vents or straws that need separate washing; if you're feeding through the night, fewer fiddly parts matters more than it sounds.
- **Wide-neck vs standard-neck** — wide-neck bottles are easier to fill and clean, which is a genuine day-to-day convenience difference rather than a feeding-performance one.
- **This is feeding equipment, not a treatment** — persistent crying, feeding problems, or poor weight gain are reasons to speak to a health visitor or GP, not just to try another bottle.
