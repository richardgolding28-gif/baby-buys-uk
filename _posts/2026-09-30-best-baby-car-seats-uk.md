- question: What does i-Size actually mean?
  answer: i-Size (UN R129) is the current EU/UK car seat safety regulation. It categorises seats by the child's height rather than weight, requires enhanced side-impact testing, and mandates rear-facing travel until at least 15 months old. It has largely superseded the older ECE R44/04 standard, though R44/04 seats already on the market can still be sold and used.
- question: Do I need the ISOFIX base, or is a seatbelt fitting enough?
  answer: A seatbelt-only fitting is legal and can be perfectly safe if done correctly every single time, but ISOFIX removes the main source of installation error by clicking the seat into fixed points built into the car. If your budget allows it, an ISOFIX base is generally the easier and more consistently secure option, especially for anyone who isn't confident checking a seatbelt route each time.
- question: How long should my baby stay rear-facing?
  answer: UK and EU guidance (and i-Size regulation specifically) recommends rear-facing travel until at least 15 months old, and many extended rear-facing seats keep children rear-facing well beyond that. Rear-facing is significantly safer for a young child's head, neck, and spine in a collision, so it's worth prioritising a seat that keeps that option open for as long as practical rather than switching forward-facing at the earliest legal opportunity.

A car seat that's the wrong fit for your car, or fitted incorrectly, undermines the one job it has. Here's what actually matters when choosing one, and which ones are worth a closer look.

## What actually matters

- **i-Size (UN R129) compliance** — the current UK/EU safety standard, which tests side-impact protection more rigorously than the older ECE R44/04 standard and requires rear-facing travel until at least 15 months.
- **Confirmed fit with your car** — not every seat fits every car, particularly with ISOFIX; check the manufacturer's vehicle compatibility list or have it fitted in-store before relying on it.
- **Rear-facing for as long as possible** — rear-facing is safer for a young child's head and neck in a crash, so a seat that supports extended rear-facing travel is worth prioritising over one that switches forward-facing early.
- **ISOFIX vs seatbelt installation** — ISOFIX bases reduce the chance of installation error compared with routing a seatbelt correctly every time, though a correctly seatbelt-fitted seat is still fully safe.
- **Never buy secondhand without a full history** — a car seat with any unknown crash history, or with expired or missing parts, should not be trusted; buy new or from someone you know the full history of.
