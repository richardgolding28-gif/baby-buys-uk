faq:
- question: What does "hip-healthy" or "IHDI certified" actually mean?
  answer: It means the carrier supports the baby's legs in an M-shape position (knees higher than bottom), which is the position paediatric hip specialists recommend for healthy hip development. Worth checking for specifically on any carrier used regularly.
- question: What age can I start babywearing?
  answer: Most structured carriers are usable from birth with a newborn insert or the carrier's built-in newborn setting, though a stretchy wrap is often more comfortable for the very early weeks. Always check the specific carrier's stated minimum weight and age.
- question: Front-facing-out, or facing the wearer?
  answer: Facing the wearer is recommended for longer, especially for younger babies, since it supports the spine better and reduces overstimulation. Forward-facing-out is fine for older, more alert babies for shorter periods once they have good head and neck control.

A carrier that isn't properly ergonomic causes real discomfort for both baby and parent within weeks of regular use. Here's what to check, and which ones consistently work well.

## What actually matters

- **Ergonomic, hip-healthy positioning** — an M-shaped leg position with knees higher than the bottom, not a narrow-based "crotch-dangle" design.
- **Lumbar support for the wearer** — padded, adjustable waist support matters a lot if you'll be wearing your baby for long stretches.
- **Weight range** — check both the minimum (some need a newborn insert) and maximum, since outgrowing a carrier early is a common complaint.
- **Breathable fabric** — genuinely matters for warmer months and for babies who run hot.
