- question: Is a bath seat safe to leave my baby unattended in?
  answer: No — bath seats and supports are aids, not safety devices, and babies should never be left unattended in the bath even for a moment, regardless of what any product claims. Always keep your baby within arm's reach.
- question: At what age can I stop using bath support?
  answer: Most babies need support until they can sit up confidently and steadily on their own, typically somewhere around 6-9 months, though this varies a lot by baby. Watch your baby's own stability rather than going by age alone.
- question: Suction cups or a stand — which is better?
  answer: Suction cup seats sit inside your existing bathtub and are more compact to store; standalone tubs with stands free up your bathroom bathtub but take more storage space. Suction only works reliably on smooth, non-textured bath surfaces, worth checking against your own bath.

A bath seat that slides around or doesn't actually hold a wriggling baby securely defeats the purpose, and makes bathtime more stressful than it needs to be. Here's what to check — and the safety point that matters more than any feature.

## What actually matters

- **Genuinely non-slip base** — suction cups that actually grip, not just ones that look like they should.
- **Secure side support** — should hold a baby upright even when they lean or reach, not just sit them centrally.
- **Easy to clean and dry** — mould and mildew build up fast in bath products left damp; look for designs without hard-to-reach crevices.
- **Never a substitute for supervision** — worth repeating: no bath seat replaces staying within arm's reach at all times.
